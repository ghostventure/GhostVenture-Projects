Product policy · reviewed November 8, 2026

Browser extensions are your choice.

Extensions can add features, and they can also access or change browser data. Use them only when you understand who made them and what access they need.

This page describes the current Ghost Surfer desktop releases. It is general product guidance, not legal advice or a guarantee that an extension is safe.

Availability by platform

The Windows and Linux builds support optional local extension imports. Imported extensions start disabled so you can review them before enabling them. This does not provide Chrome Web Store compatibility. The macOS preview does not include the desktop extension feature. Check the Downloads page and FAQ for current platform details.

What an extension may access

An extension may be able to read or change information on websites, interact with tabs, or use other browser features, depending on how it was built and the access it requests. That information could include page text, form contents, account details you enter, or browsing activity. Some extensions may send data to services operated by their publishers. Ghost Surfer's built-in privacy controls do not verify or control an extension's own data practices.

Before enabling one

  • Get the extension from a source you trust and confirm who publishes it.
  • Read its description, requested permissions, privacy notice, and update policy. If you cannot find or understand them, leave it disabled.
  • Ask whether the requested access makes sense for the feature. Broad access to every site deserves extra care.
  • Avoid using extensions with sensitive accounts or information unless you trust the publisher and understand the data flow.
  • Remove extensions you no longer need, and review them again if their publisher or requested access changes.

Who is responsible?

Extensions are third-party software. Ghost Surfer does not publish, audit, certify, endorse, or warrant imported extensions. The extension's own publisher controls its code, permissions, updates, data handling, and applicable license. Review that publisher's terms and privacy information before use. If you enable an extension, you are responsible for deciding whether it is appropriate for your device, accounts, network, and local requirements.

Disable or remove an extension

Use Ghost Control's extension manager to disable an enabled extension or remove an imported extension. If the browser reports unexpected extension behavior, disable it first, close Ghost Surfer, and remove it from the extension manager. If you cannot access that control, stop using the extension and contact Support for help.

How this relates to other policies

Your browser profile is stored locally, but an enabled extension may process or transmit information under its own practices. See the Privacy Policy for Ghost Surfer's data handling and the Terms of Use for the general rules that apply to use of the software. The Security page explains the product's protections and limits.

Permitted and prohibited use

You may use extensions for lawful purposes. Do not use Ghost Surfer or an extension to steal credentials, distribute malware, conduct unauthorized access, violate another person's rights, or bypass access controls. See Acceptable Use for the full rules.
