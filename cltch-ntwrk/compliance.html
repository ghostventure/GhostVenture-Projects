Compliance

Effective Date: July 1, 2026

CLTCH.NTWRK is a U.S.-based booking and operations platform for hosts, venues, performers, and creative talent. This page explains the compliance areas we design for and the responsibilities that remain with hosts, performers, and venues.

Platform Compliance Areas

  • Account security, session protection, role-based workspace access, and database access controls.
  • Privacy rights handling for access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out requests where applicable.
  • Accessibility improvements using WCAG 2.2 AA as the practical target for public and core workflow pages.
  • DMCA notice, counter-notice, and repeat-infringer handling for profile media, portfolio links, flyers, and creative content.
  • Trust and safety workflows for misleading gigs, unsafe event conditions, profile fraud, harassment, no-shows, and payment disputes.
  • Support and legal escalation paths for urgent booking, privacy, copyright, accessibility, and safety issues.

Host and Venue Responsibilities

Hosts and venues remain responsible for event-specific legal requirements, including permits, venue licenses, age restrictions, insurance, fire and occupancy rules, supervision requirements, union or contractor obligations, taxes, and any city, county, borough, parish, or state rules that apply to the event.

Performer Responsibilities

Performers remain responsible for accurate profile information, lawful portfolio materials, professional conduct, truthful availability, required licenses or credentials for the services they offer, and compliance with any agreement, rider, or event rule they accept.

Payments and Financial Controls

Where CLTCH.NTWRK provides active payment or payout tooling, those workflows must follow the disclosed feature terms and processor requirements. Where payment is handled directly between users, the users remain responsible for the payment relationship, tax reporting, chargeback handling, invoices, and written agreements.

Not Legal Advice

This page is operational guidance, not legal advice. If you need advice for a specific event, employment classification, licensing issue, tax question, insurance requirement, or contract, consult a qualified professional.
